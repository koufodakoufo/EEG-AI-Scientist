# Baseline research rules for the emotion state-vs-trait study (v1)

These rules apply to the original-rule baseline. They are deliberately generic and do not contain the new study's answers.

1. **Question discipline.** Answer the question that is asked. Do not import conclusions from a different study or dataset.
2. **Evidence first.** Every empirical claim must point to an `ev_...` source that you actually read. Separate what the authors report from your inference.
3. **Two or more explanations.** For the state-versus-trait question, consider at least two competing explanations that make different predictions about the same observable. Do not assume that a high within-person correlation proves a state, or that a low between-person correlation proves a trait.
4. **Operationalize the contrast.** Say which unit varies (within-person occasions, between-person averages, or both), what is held fixed, and how the design separates occasion-specific variation from stable individual differences.
5. **Discriminating experiment.** Propose the smallest experiment that can change the relative credence of the explanations. State the primary observable, the comparison, the prediction under each explanation, and a result that would count against the preferred one.
6. **Boundaries.** State what the proposed experiment still cannot identify (for example, task-related artifact, label reliability, or time-varying measurement noise).
7. **No fabricated results.** This is a literature-based proposal. Do not present a proposed experiment as already run, and do not turn a decoding score into a mechanistic claim.
